Andrew Cheng

I’m a PhD student at Harvard University, advised by Yue Lu.

My research connects machine learning, optimization, and statistics. My recent work focuses on enabling fast decision-making for foundation models on unstructured data.

My earlier work explored Riemannian optimization and high-dimensional learning dynamics. I also developed interpretable models with provable guarantees for large-scale tabular data, applying them to problems in national security and healthcare.

Exact Dynamics and Finite-Sample Trajectory Recovery of Linear Recursive Feature Machines

Andrew Cheng, Bobak T. Kiani, Yue M. Lu, Adityanarayanan Radhakrishnan

We characterize how an iterative method alternating ridge regression with average gradient outer product (AGOP) updates learns task-relevant features for multi-output regression. We explain how these features evolve and how much data is needed to reliably recover their learning trajectory.

NeurIPS OPT Workshop2024

High Dimensional First Order Mini-Batch Algorithms on Quadratic Problems

Andrew Cheng, Kiwon Lee, Courtney Paquette

We characterize high-dimensional mini-batch learning dynamics for regularized least squares. A deterministic discrete Volterra equation describes quadratic statistics of SGD, stochastic Nesterov, and momentum methods.

Technical details

Under the assumptions in the paper, quadratic statistics \( \mathcal{R}: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R} \), including training loss and excess risk, concentrate around deterministic predictions as sample size and dimension grow. The analysis concerns \( \ell_2 \)-regularized least-squares problems.

Trajectory of Mini-Batch Momentum: Batch Size Saturation and Convergence in High Dimensions

Kiwon Lee, Andrew Cheng, Elliot Paquette, Courtney Paquette

We describe how batch size controls momentum dynamics on high-dimensional least-squares problems. An implicit conditioning ratio identifies when mini-batch momentum can match the accelerated convergence rate of full-batch momentum.
